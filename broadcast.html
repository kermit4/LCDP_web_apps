  How broadcasting works

  

    This page records your camera/mic and pushes the video to your local cjp2p node in
    real time. Here is the full path from your camera to a viewer:
  

  
    
    
  1. 
      Record & chunk: your browser captures video and audio via MediaRecorder,
      yielding binary chunks roughly every second. Each chunk is stored in IndexedDB in your browser.
    
    2. 
    
  2. 
      Push blocks: chunks are sliced into 4 KB blocks and sent to your local node
      over a WebSocket as Content messages with a byte offset.
      The node signs each block with your ed25519 private key before storing it.
    
    3. 
    
  3. 
      Cue file: a parallel .cues.json sidecar stream is also pushed,
      recording which byte offsets correspond to keyframes and their timestamps.
      This is what lets the watch page seek to arbitrary points in a live or finished recording.
    
    4. 
    
  4. 
      Viewers pull: viewers open the stream URL
      (/stream/<pubkey>/<stream-id>) and request blocks they don't yet have.
      Your node serves blocks directly from its signed store.
    
    5. 
    
  5. 
      Viewers re-serve: once a viewer has downloaded a block,
      their node stores it locally and will answer other viewers' requests for the same block.
      The broadcaster's node is no longer the sole source — every viewer is also a partial server.
      As the viewer count grows, so does the total available bandwidth, because each new viewer
      adds upload capacity for the blocks it has already received.
    
    6. 
    
  6. 
      Authenticity: blocks carry ed25519 signatures, so viewers can verify
      that every byte came from the original broadcaster. A malicious peer that tries to serve
      modified bytes will be rejected.
    
    7. 
  


  Offline resilience:
  after a live broadcast ends, any peer that downloaded the stream can continue serving it.
  The stream lives in the network as long as at least one peer holds the blocks — not
  just while your node is running.
